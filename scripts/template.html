What counts as broken

Documented breach means the treaty was violated, repudiated or abrogated before its intended end. For alliances 1815–2018 this is the ATOP project's own coding; for peace and security treaties it is a curated record with a dated event and a source.

War between signatories means two parties fought each other in an interstate war that began within the chosen window after signing. For peace treaties only former enemies count; for alliances any two members count, whether or not the alliance was still in force.

Historian-flagged cases

Some treaties are widely argued to have set up a later war without being breached directly (Versailles and the Second World War, Frankfurt 1871 and the First). These are interpretations, kept separate from the counts and shown in violet with a one-line rationale.

“Major” wars are great-power wars or those with roughly 10,000+ battle deaths.

Sources and gaps

Alliances: ATOP 5.1 (Leeds et al.), covering 1815–2018. Wars 1816–2003: Correlates of War inter-state war data v4.0. Peace treaties, pre-1816 alliances and wars, and wars after 2003 are hand-curated from standard references, so coverage before 1815 is limited to major treaties and is not comparable with the later data.

Alliances signed after 2018 are not included. Civil wars and wars against non-state groups are outside the scope.

Each line is a Kaplan–Meier survival curve: the share of treaties for which the outcome has not yet happened, by years since signing. Treaties whose signatories ceased to exist, or that are too recent to have been observed for long, drop out of the denominator instead of counting as “kept”. Shaded bands are 95% confidence intervals; a curve stops once fewer than five treaties remain under observation.

Allies are pairs with an ATOP agreement in force that year. Enemies are pairs in an active strategic rivalry (Thompson, Sakuwa & Suhas) or fighting an interstate war that year. A pair that is both allied and rivals counts as enemies, because the rivalry is the stronger signal; this is how Austria and Italy appear inside the Triple Alliance. Three-way rivalries (all three pairs enemies) are left out of the share, since the theory treats them as ambiguous. The great-power panel uses the Correlates of War list of major powers for each year.
